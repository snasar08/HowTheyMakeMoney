Samsung’s $395 smart glasses are up for preorder, but there’s no display

 Nov 15 2026

 Chris Steven

 Add MIXED on Google
 Choose MIXED as a preferred source in Google Search.

 Preferred Source

Samsung and Google’s Intelligent Eyewear is finally available to preorder, with prices starting at $395 in the United States. The smart glasses arrive on December 10, and there are two collections to choose from: Warby Parker and Gentle Monster.

Before you order, there is a difference worth knowing. These glasses do not put a screen in front of your eyes. Samsung says you interact with them through voice, touch controls and audio instead, with a camera to help Gemini understand what you are looking at.

They also need a compatible phone. If you already have a supported Galaxy, Pixel or iPhone with the required software, you do not need to replace it just to use the glasses. Check your exact model and software version before making a preorder.

Samsung shows the six Gentle Monster frame designs in its launch announcement. Image: Samsung / Gentle Monster

How much the glasses cost and where they are launching

Samsung says Warby Parker Intelligent Eyewear starts at $395 in the US, while Gentle Monster models cost $449 to $489 depending on the design. Preorders opened on November 12 through select channels. Our earlier coverage described the glasses before prices and final launch details were announced.

Both collections are coming to the US, and Gentle Monster’s collection is also launching in South Korea. Samsung says the two brands offer a combined 30 variations across their frame designs. Prescription options are available through the retailers; check lens choices and their costs before ordering.

Warby Parker’s designs come with a choice of optical and sun lenses. Image: Samsung / Warby Parker

What they can do without a screen

The glasses combine a 12-megapixel camera, open-ear speakers and microphones. Samsung says you can ask Gemini about what you see, listen to notifications and get voice-guided directions or translation. Availability can vary by service, language and region.

There is a camera indicator for people nearby. Samsung says the camera is designed to stop capturing if the external light is covered or not working. The company also says the product is intended for adults aged 18 and over, with age verification required for activation and app downloads.

Samsung rates the glasses for up to nine hours on a charge and up to 72 hours with a fully charged case. Those are manufacturer estimates, so actual time will depend on how you use them.

Google’s promotional image shows a close-up of the glasses’ camera. Image: Google / Samsung / Warby Parker

Which phones can connect to Samsung’s Intelligent Eyewear?

Samsung lists Galaxy S, S FE and supported Z series phones running One UI 9.0 or later; Pixel 7 series and later on Android 17 with the September 2026 update or newer; and iPhone 13 or later models with at least 6GB of RAM and iOS 26.6 or later. Not every phone in those broad families necessarily meets every requirement, so I would check the precise model before ordering.

If you are already shopping for a phone, the unlocked Galaxy S26 is an example from Samsung’s supported Galaxy S family when running One UI 9.0 or later. The phone and eyewear are separate purchases, and an existing compatible phone is enough.
