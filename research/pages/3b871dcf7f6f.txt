James Harden scores 51 to pass Kobe Bryant on the all time list, Indianapolis cuts Daniel Jones eight months after his extension, and Denver outlasts a Sengun triple double.

Morning board

NBA: James Harden scored 51 points in Cleveland’s 128 to 119 NBA Cup win over Indiana, his 26th career 50 point game, passing Kobe Bryant for third place all time. Franz Wagner hit the winning shot in Orlando’s 113 to 111 win at Brooklyn, Scottie Barnes hit the go ahead layup in Toronto’s 105 to 102 win at Detroit, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 40 in Oklahoma City’s comeback win over New Orleans, and Denver beat Houston 133 to 124 behind Nikola Jokic’s second half takeover, with Alperen Sengun’s triple double on the losing side.

NFL: Indianapolis released Daniel Jones on Friday, less than a day after the 26 to 3 loss at Houston and eight months after his two year, 88 million dollar extension, and Anthony Richardson starts on November 29 against the New York Giants. Sean McVay confirmed Puka Nacua is likely out for the season after opting for MCL surgery. Sunday’s gates are mostly settled, Kenneth Walker III is out, Baker Mayfield is questionable and trending in, and Brock Bowers and Carson Beck remain open until Sunday.

MLB: The non tender deadline turned 44 players into free agents on Friday night, Minnesota traded Royce Lewis to the Los Angeles Dodgers for Bobby Miller and Francisco Espinoza rather than non tender him, and the first reported offer in the Tarik Skubal market is in, nine years and almost 400 million dollars from the New York Mets, with a decision still expected before the bargaining agreement expires on December 1.

Last night’s standouts

NBA: Harden’s historic night leads a Cup slate full of closers

Cleveland beat Indiana 128 to 119 in NBA Cup play, and James Harden owned the night. Harden scored a season high 51 points on 15 of 26 shooting with seven three pointers and a perfect 14 of 14 at the line, adding six rebounds and six assists in 40 minutes. It was his 26th career 50 point game, passing Kobe Bryant for third place all time, and his first 50 point game for Cleveland. He had 42 points through three quarters, Cleveland won the third quarter 43 to 22, and when Indiana cut the lead to six late, Harden closed it himself with a three pointer and two free throws. Donovan Mitchell added 20 points, and Pascal Siakam scored 21 for Indiana. Fantasy watch: Harden had been averaging 15.6 points over his last 10 games, so this is a ceiling reminder rather than a new baseline, and the sell high window on a 51 point night with the history attached is as wide as it will ever be.

Denver beat Houston 133 to 124 in the night’s best game, a Cup battle that changed leads 32 times, the most in the league this season. Nikola Jokic scored 24 of his 26 points after halftime, 18 of them in the third quarter, and finished with 11 assists and seven rebounds. Alperen Sengun answered with 28 points, 11 rebounds and 10 assists, his second triple double of the season, playing through a black eye and a scratched cornea after starting the game in goggles and ditching them before the first quarter ended. Houston led by five with just over five minutes left before Denver closed the game out. Fred VanVleet scored 25, Amen Thompson 22 and Jabari Smith Jr. 21 for Houston. Fantasy watch: Sengun was questionable all day with the eye injuries and produced a triple double anyway, so the streaming big contingency in Houston is off and he is a full go, and Jokic turning two first half points into a takeover is the reminder that his quiet halves do not stay quiet.

Orlando won 113 to 111 at Brooklyn, and Franz Wagner closed it. Wagner scored 35 points with five rebounds and seven assists, and his step back jumper with the game tied at 111 in the final seconds was the winner. Desmond Bane added 21 points with seven three pointers, and Julius Randle scored 27 with nine rebounds and six assists for Brooklyn. Fantasy watch: Wagner is Orlando’s closer while Paolo Banchero is out, measured in weeks rather than days, and 35 points with the last shot is what that usage looks like when it lands.

Toronto won 105 to 102 at Detroit, snapping a six game losing streak in the game it played without RJ Barrett and Collin Murray-Boyles. Scottie Barnes scored 29 points on 14 of 21 shooting with five rebounds and five assists, and his driving layup in the final seconds put Toronto ahead for good after Cade Cunningham had given Detroit the lead. Cunningham scored 23 points with nine assists in his return after sitting Wednesday, Toronto rookie Allen Graves added 20 points and 10 rebounds in his first career start, and Immanuel Quickley had 18 points and 10 assists for Toronto. Fantasy watch: Barnes carried a short handed lineup to an efficient ceiling game, and Cunningham’s workload was full in his first game back, which settles the return question in one night.

Oklahoma City rallied past New Orleans 126 to 120, trailing by eight at halftime and winning the fourth quarter 34 to 22. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 40 points to lead the comeback. The frontcourt line belonged to a rookie, as Aday Mara scored 22 points with 16 rebounds and two blocks in a thinned Oklahoma City rotation. Trey Murphy III returned for New Orleans with 17 points in 29 minutes after missing six straight games, playing on a minutes restriction. Fantasy watch: Mara producing starter lines while the frontcourt is thin is a streaming case that builds with every game like this, and Murphy’s shot was there in capped minutes, so the workload ramp is the only question left on his return.

Washington won 98 to 94 at Chicago, taking control with a 23 to 9 third quarter. Anthony Davis scored 36 points with 11 rebounds on 16 of 26 shooting, and AJ Dybantsa added 20 points, 10 rebounds and six assists. Fantasy watch: Davis carried a road comeback as Washington’s frontcourt anchor, a ceiling game from a player whose managers have waited on exactly this shape of night.

Atlanta beat Charlotte 111 to 97 behind Jalen Johnson, who scored 26 points with six rebounds, six assists and four steals after Atlanta trailed by 14 early. Phoenix won 111 to 101 at Utah, where Devin Booker scored 23 points and closed the game late, and Darryn Peterson ran Utah’s offence to 20 points, 10 rebounds and eight assists, a near triple double, with Jaxson Hayes adding 18 points and 14 rebounds. The Los Angeles Lakers edged Sacramento 106 to 104, as Luka Doncic scored 25 points with eight assists, Walker Kessler added 15 points, seven rebounds and three blocks, De’Andre Hunter scored 30 for Sacramento and Domantas Sabonis had 19 points and 16 rebounds in the loss. Fantasy watch: Peterson’s usage is the Utah name to track if it holds, and Kessler’s three blocks are the streaming relevant line on the Lakers’ side.

NHL: A dark day, and Saturday carries everything

The league played no games on Friday, a planned dark day in the schedule, and the news was all about Saturday’s 14 game slate. Pittsburgh placed Nicholas Robertson on injured reserve with a lower body injury, to be re evaluated in two weeks, and Evgeni Malkin is out for the Penguins’ two game road trip, while Artemi Panarin is expected to return for Los Angeles against Ottawa after missing three games. Quinn Hughes remains day to day for Minnesota’s game at Vancouver tonight, and Connor Bedard remains out for Chicago at Calgary with his rehabilitation against surgery decision still unannounced. Both files are covered in full in the Movers below.

MLB: The deadline reshaped the market in one night

Friday’s 5:00 p.m. ET non tender deadline turned 44 players into free agents, and the names carry real fantasy weight. Bailey Ober, Trevor Larnach and Matt Wallner came out of Minnesota, Luis Gil and Fernando Cruz out of New York, with Nolan Gorman, David Fry, Davis Schneider, Matt Vierling, Kyle Isbel, Josiah Gray, Luke Raley and JP Sears also reaching the open market. Gil was the American League Rookie of the Year in 2024 and pitched limited innings in 2026 around right shoulder inflammation. Minnesota also traded Royce Lewis to the Dodgers hours before the deadline rather than non tender him, and the Yankees agreed with Jonathan Loaisiga on a two year deal worth about 18 million dollars, pending a physical, after his 2.42 earned run average in 67 appearances for Arizona. Fantasy watch: a former Rookie of the Year and a rotation arm with Ober’s track record hitting the market on the same night deepens the free agent pool, and every landing spot decided in the next 11 days moves a keeper price, with the bargaining agreement expiring December 1 and a lockout able to freeze transactions from December 2 for an unknown duration.

Movers

Indianapolis released Daniel Jones, and Richardson gets the next start What happened: The Colts announced on Friday that they have released Jones, less than a day after he went 12 of 27 for 144 yards with an interception in the 26 to 3 loss at Houston. Jones signed a two year, 88 million dollar extension in March with 50 million dollars fully guaranteed. He did not request the release, and the club described the move as a decision made in the best interest of both parties. Richardson, the fourth overall pick in 2023, makes his first start since the 2024 season on November 29 against the New York Giants, and Jones sits on waivers until Monday at 4:00 p.m. ET, with a claiming team taking on just over two million dollars for the rest of 2026 and 38 million dollars in non guaranteed money for 2027. Why it matters: Indianapolis paid starter money in March and cut its starter in November, and every Colts skill player is now valued inside a Richardson offence that has not started a game in two seasons. Reporting around the move points to a 10 million dollar injury guarantee as one factor in the timing, and Richardson gets another opportunity to establish himself as the starter. What to do: Richardson is a stash, not a start, because Indianapolis does not play until November 29. Hold Colts pass catchers through the extended break and reassess after Richardson’s first start. Jones has no fantasy home until a claiming team or a new signing gives him one, and Monday’s waiver deadline is the first checkpoint.

Puka Nacua is likely out for the season, and the Rams’ targets consolidate What happened: McVay said Friday that Nacua has opted for MCL surgery and is expected to miss a couple of months, likely the rest of the season. Nacua was already on injured reserve with MCL and PCL sprains and turf toe from the November 8 game at Washington, and he finishes 2026 with 70 catches for 715 yards and four touchdowns in seven games. Why it matters: The question hanging over the Rams’ passing game since November 8 is answered. Until Davante Adams returns from injured reserve, the targets consolidate to a committee with no must start name in it, and the last Nacua less game showed exactly that shape, spread snaps, routes and targets with no single receiver separating. What to do: Nacua is droppable in redraft formats. In dynasty and keeper formats he is a hold at his injured price. Do not chase the committee as a group, and treat any single Rams receiver as a matchup play until Adams is back and the shape changes.

Sunday’s gates are mostly settled, and Bowers is the one that stays open What happened: Friday’s final injury reports settled most of the week. Kenneth Walker III is out for Kansas City against Arizona after missing all three practices with a groin injury, his second straight game out, and Emmett Johnson starts again behind a line also missing its starting right guard and right tackle. Baker Mayfield is questionable for Tampa Bay at Detroit after limited practices on Thursday and Friday, with his head coach saying he had a good day on Friday and is trending in the right direction. Dalton Kincaid is doubtful for Buffalo against Miami after missing the whole week, and Ladd McConkey is cleared with no game designation for the Chargers against the Jets after missing two games. Brock Bowers is questionable for Las Vegas at Denver after getting heavy work in Friday’s practice, and Bowers himself said his availability remains up in the air. Carson Beck is questionable for Arizona at Kansas City after not practising Friday, and Arizona is proceeding as if Josh Johnson, signed under two weeks ago, will make the start. In San Francisco, Mike Evans is questionable against Minnesota with his Achilles, and the 49ers make their call today. Why it matters: Walker is out and McConkey is cleared; Kincaid is doubtful but has not been ruled out, and Mayfield leans in. Bowers and Beck are the two Sunday lineups still genuinely open, and both sit at premium positions, tight end and quarterback, where the contingency has to be owned before Sunday morning rather than found after inactives post. What to do: Start Johnson for Kansas City with the line caveat noted. Have Dawson Knox ready as the Buffalo tight end if Kincaid sits, as expected. Hold Bowers and Michael Mayer plans until Sunday inactives, about 90 minutes before kickoff, and downgrade every Arizona pass catcher if Johnson starts at Arrowhead. Watch for the Evans decision today, and treat an active Evans as an in game management risk rather than a full workload.

Jerami Grant has a calf strain, and Memphis loses two rotation pieces on the same day What happened: Grant has been diagnosed with a left calf strain and will not play today at Minnesota. He left Thursday’s win over Golden State after five minutes and did not return, and no return date beyond today has been given. Memphis also loses Cameron Boozer for the first game of his career today, after he sprained his right ankle in Thursday’s win. Why it matters: Thursday’s early exit now has a diagnosis attached, and calf strains carry open ended timelines as a class, so a one game ruling today is a floor rather than a timetable. Jaylen Wells and Cedric Coward absorbed the wing minutes on Thursday, and that shape is the rotation until Grant is re evaluated. What to do: Grant is a hold with no drop attached on a one game ruling. Wells and Coward are the streaming answers in Memphis for today’s game at Minnesota, and the next checkpoint is Memphis’s reporting ahead of its next game, not a date anyone has announced.

Hughes’s availability and Bedard’s treatment decision remain open What happened: Hughes remains day to day with an upper body injury after leaving Thursday’s game in Calgary in the third period, and no ruling on tonight’s game at Vancouver had landed by this morning. His 13 game point streak, tied for the longest active streak in the league, is on the line if he plays. Bedard remains out for Chicago’s game at Calgary today and the decision between rehabilitation and surgery on his right shoulder is still unannounced, with the reported figures standing at roughly six weeks for rehabilitation with a high risk of reinjury and roughly three to four months for surgery. Artemi Panarin is expected to return for Los Angeles against Ottawa after missing three games, and Pittsburgh placed Nicholas Robertson on injured reserve, to be reevaluated in two weeks, with Evgeni Malkin also out for the Penguins’ road trip. Why it matters: Hughes and Bedard are the two biggest open files in the league. Hughes’s game status is pending, while Chicago has not announced Bedard’s treatment decision. Panarin’s expected return is the day’s one clear lineup gain, and Pittsburgh’s absences open minutes on a Penguins team already travelling. What to do: The morning skate and pregame reporting in Vancouver are the first real Hughes signals, so hold any replacement move until they land. Bedard stays undroppable and untradeable at a discount until Chicago announces the decision. If Panarin is confirmed in the lineup against Ottawa, he is a start in every format.

The first Skubal number is in, and it is only the opening bid What happened: Reporting on Friday put the Mets’ initial offer to Tarik Skubal at nine years and almost 400 million dollars, a figure that would be the highest average annual value ever for a free agent starting pitcher. A fifth team met Skubal by video call on Wednesday, joining the Mets, the Dodgers, the Blue Jays and the Padres among the clubs that have met him. There is no signing, and the expectation around the league remains that the top of the market signs before the December 1 bargaining deadline rather than waiting out a possible freeze. Why it matters: The winter’s biggest free agent file now has a price attached, and it is an opening bid with other offers expected to follow. Where Skubal lands sets the top of the 2027 pitching market and reprices every ace behind him in keeper and dynasty formats. What to do: Nothing yet. There is no signing to act on, and the reported offer is a marker, not a destination. The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, and a possible lockout could delay an unresolved signing. Any agreement before it is the trigger for repricing, not the reporting of the first number.
