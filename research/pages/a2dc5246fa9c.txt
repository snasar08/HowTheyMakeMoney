Roblox Q3 DAUs Surge 70% to 151.5M and Free Cash Flow Hits $443M

RRBLX•2026-01-08

Roblox's Q3 daily active users rose 70% year-over-year to 151.5 million, driving revenue up 48% to $1.36 billion and bookings to $1.92 billion. Operating cash flow climbed 121% to $546 million, producing $443 million in free cash flow while GAAP net loss widened to $257 million.

1. Accelerating User and Revenue Growth

Roblox reported a surge in platform engagement during its third quarter, with daily active users reaching 151.5 million, a 70% year-over-year increase, and total hours engaged climbing 95% to 39.6 billion. This translated into a 48% rise in revenue to $1.36 billion. The company’s bookings—representing the value of virtual currency purchases used on the platform—jumped 70% to $1.92 billion, underscoring strong monetization alongside user growth. These figures mark an acceleration from the prior quarter, when user growth was 41%, hours engaged rose 58%, revenue increased 21% and bookings advanced 51%.

2. Robust Cash Generation Despite GAAP Losses

Roblox’s third quarter operating cash flow more than doubled, climbing 121% year-over-year to $546 million, while free cash flow rose 103% to $443 million. For the full year of 2025, management expects free cash flow in the range of $1.10 billion to $1.13 billion. However, on an accounting basis the company recorded a net loss of $257 million in Q3—worse than the $240 million loss in the year-ago period—and has guided for a fourth-quarter loss of between $345 million and $375 million, which would bring full-year net losses to approximately $1.1 billion. The discrepancy between strong cash generation and large reported losses is driven largely by $831 million in stock-based compensation over the nine months ended September 30 and continued investment in platform safety and research and development.

3. Valuation and Investor Takeaways

With a market capitalization near $51 billion and investors effectively paying around 12 times forward sales, Roblox’s valuation assumes sustained high growth and a path to GAAP profitability. The company has signaled potential headwinds in 2026, including more challenging year-over-year comparisons, unpredictable emergence of viral experiences, and the short-term impact of enhanced safety features on engagement. Management has emphasized its willingness to prioritize long-term platform growth and safety over near-term results, indicating that operating margins could decline modestly next year as investments continue. While the long-term opportunity remains substantial—management still targets capturing 10% of the global gaming market—the current valuation leaves little margin for execution missteps or slower-than-expected growth.
